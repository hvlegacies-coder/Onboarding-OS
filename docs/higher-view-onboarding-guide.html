Section 1

The short version

Higher View recruits tax preparers. This platform takes over everything that happens between "I'm interested" and "I'm trained and working" — so the people doing the recruiting can spend their time on relationships instead of paperwork.

The problem it solves

Recruiting a preparer used to mean a long chain of manual steps: get their details, book them into an info session, remember to remind them, send the right contract with the right logo on it, chase them when they don't sign, tell someone when they've gone quiet, welcome them, book their orientation, and get them into training. Miss one step and a good candidate quietly disappears.

What the platform does instead

An Office Owner shares one link. Everything after that runs on its own:

  • The person fills in a short form and picks an info session.
  • They get a confirmation, a calendar invite, and reminders before the session.
  • About an hour after the session, their contract arrives — already branded with the correct office's logo and business name.
  • If they don't sign, the system nudges them twice, then stops and tells the owner to make a personal call.
  • When they sign, they're welcomed, booked into orientation, and given training access automatically.

Why it's built to be sold, not just used

Every office is kept separate — its own link, its own logo, its own contract — while sharing one engine underneath. That's the same shape as a product you could license to any company with multiple offices or franchises. Higher View is simply the first one using it.

The one-sentence summary

One link per office, one standard form for everyone, and an automated path that carries a recruit from first contact to fully trained — with the right contract every single time.

Section 2

Who uses it

Three kinds of people touch this system, and they see completely different things.

1. The Prospect (the person being recruited)

They see exactly one page, ever: a short sign-up form reached through the link their recruiter sent them. No login, no account, no menus. They fill in their name, phone, email, and pick which info session they want to attend. After that, everything reaches them by email and text.

Importantly, this form looks identical no matter which office invited them. No logos, no office names, no dropdown asking who referred them to which branch. The system already knows, because it's baked into the link.

2. The Office Owner

A leader who recruits preparers into their own office. They log in and see one page — My Office — showing every person they've invited, where each one stands, and whether their contract is signed. They also get their shareable link right at the top.

They only ever see their own people. An owner cannot see another office's prospects, numbers, or contracts.

3. The Platform Operator (Higher View admin)

The Higher View team running the whole thing. They see everything: the full funnel across all offices, every prospect, every contract, all the offices and their branding status, session schedules, and the library of automated messages.

Section 3

What an Office Owner actually has to do

Three things. That's the promise the platform makes.

1
Invite Send your link to someone you'd like to recruit. Text it, email it, post it — it doesn't matter. Anyone who signs up through your link is automatically yours.
2
Build the relationship Coach them. Answer their questions. Be the human in the process while the system handles the scheduling, the paperwork, and the reminders.
3
Support them Once they're signed and onboarded, train them on how your office specifically works.

The one moment the system hands something back to you

If someone doesn't sign their contract after two automatic reminders, the system stops messaging them and flags them on your dashboard as "Needs your call." That's deliberate. After two nudges, more automated messages just feel like spam — what's needed is a real conversation. That's your job, and the platform tells you exactly when it's time.

Section 4

The journey, step by step

Here's the entire path a recruit travels. The tag on each step tells you who does the work.

Owner the office owner   Higher View the Higher View team   Automatic nobody — the system does it

1
The invitation Owner The owner shares their personal link. The moment someone opens it, the system already knows which office they belong to — the recruit never has to say, and can never pick wrong.
2
Signing up Automatic The recruit fills in name, phone, email, and chooses one of the published info session dates. They can add who referred them, but it's optional. They immediately get a confirmation email and text with a calendar invite and the meeting link. The owner gets a notification that someone registered.
3
Reminders before the session Automatic Reminders go out 24 hours before, 2 hours before, and 30 minutes before (that last one includes the meeting link). If somebody signs up an hour before the session starts, they simply get the 30-minute reminder and nothing that's already passed.
4
The Discovery Session Higher View A live session run by Higher View covering the company, the opportunity, compensation, expectations, the contractor relationship, and training — then questions. Attendance is recorded, because that's what triggers the contract.
5
The contract arrives Automatic Roughly 30–60 minutes after the session ends, the recruit receives their Independent Contractor Agreement — carrying the inviting owner's logo, business name, and contract template. If that office's branding hasn't been set up yet, the system deliberately sends nothing and alerts an admin instead. A wrong contract is worse than a late one.
6
Two nudges, then a human Automatic If it isn't signed, a first reminder goes out, then a final one. Still unsigned after that, automation stops completely and the owner is notified with the recruit's full details and a simple instruction: reach out personally and find out whether they're still interested.
7
They sign Automatic A congratulations message goes out, their New Preparer Orientation gets scheduled, and their Training Community access is granted automatically using the signed contract — no more uploading anything to a website by hand.
8
New Preparer Orientation Higher View A separate session from the Discovery Session — this one is pure onboarding: the training community, the portal, required modules, required meetings, support, and expectations. No contract talk. These two sessions are never combined.
9
Welcome packet and training Automatic They receive their training community link, their checklist, contacts, and FAQ — and their required modules are assigned: Due Diligence, Filing Statuses (Single, MFJ, MFS, HOH, QSS), and Intro Tax Training.
10
Handed to the owner Owner Coaching begins. The automated part of the journey is complete.

The stages you'll see on screen

Invitation sent → Discovery scheduled → Reminder sent → Attended → Contract sent → Reminder #1 → Reminder #2 → then either Signed or Owner follow-up needed → Orientation scheduled → Orientation completed → Training access sent → Modules assigned → Coaching begins.

Section 5

The screens

The sign-up form — what a recruit sees

Reached at a web address ending in /join/ followed by the office's name. It asks for four things and offers the available session dates. That's the entire prospect-facing experience. If a link is broken or doesn't match a real office, the recruit sees a polite "this link isn't valid, please ask for a new one" message rather than a form that would file them under nobody.

The login page

Owners and Higher View staff sign in here, choosing whether they're signing in as an Admin or an Office Owner. Recruits never log in at all.

My Office — the Office Owner's page

This is the whole product for an owner. Top to bottom:

  • Your invite link — with a copy button and a preview button so you can see exactly what your recruits see.
  • People who need your call — a red banner listing anyone whose contract stalled, with their phone and email one tap away. If nobody's stalled, the banner doesn't appear.
  • Four counters — how many have no contract yet, how many are awaiting signature, how many have signed, and how many need a personal call.
  • Everyone you've invited — one card per person showing their contact details, which session they're booked into, their current stage, their contract status, and a progress bar of how far along the journey they are. Each card ends with a plain-English line explaining what happens next.
  • Filters — tap any status to narrow the list.

Section 6

The five rules that must never break

These aren't preferences. If any one of them slips, the platform stops being trustworthy.

1 One standardized form. Every office uses the identical sign-up form. No logos, no branding, no dropdown asking which office. Why it matters: the moment offices get custom forms, you're maintaining six forms instead of one, and adding a seventh office becomes a project instead of a checkbox.
2 The link decides the office — never the recruit. Office attribution comes from the unique link and nothing else. Why it matters: people pick the wrong item from dropdowns constantly. A misattributed recruit gets the wrong contract and lands on the wrong owner's dashboard.
3 All contacts live in one central account. Not scattered across per-office systems. Why it matters: one place to look, one funnel to measure, no records lost between systems.
4 The right contract, every single time. The contract must carry the inviting owner's logo, business name, and template. If those aren't on file, the system sends nothing and raises an alert. Why it matters: sending someone a contract with another company's name on it is the kind of mistake that costs you the recruit.
5 The two sessions are different things. The Discovery Session is the info session for people deciding. The New Preparer Orientation is onboarding for people who already signed. Why it matters: merging them means either boring signed preparers with a sales pitch, or overwhelming undecided prospects with onboarding detail.

The four things blocking go-live

  1. Collect every office's contract, logo, and business name. Only one office is complete today. Until all of them are on file, contracts cannot send automatically — and the platform will correctly refuse to send rather than send the wrong one.
  2. Confirm the shared calendar and the session times.
  3. Connect the contract platform so it can receive which office a recruit belongs to and report back when they've signed.
  4. Connect the training community so access is granted the moment someone signs.

One security note worth understanding

Right now the login passwords are stored inside the app itself, which means anyone technical enough could read them. That's completely normal for a prototype and fine for demos — but those accounts must move to a proper secure server before any real recruit's details go into the system. Treat today's passwords as demo passwords, and don't reuse them anywhere that matters.
